# Week 1 Project Scope — FinAI Guide

| Field | Project Details |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Project Name** | FinAI Guide: A RAG-Based Financial Education Assistant for Malaysian Young Adults |
| **Domain** | Financial Education / Everyday Money Management in Malaysia |
| **Problem Statement** | Malaysian students and young adults may struggle to find and understand reliable explanations of budgeting, saving and emergency funds. Useful information is available in financial education documents, but users must locate relevant sections and connect them to their questions. FinAI Guide will use Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to retrieve relevant passages and explain them in clear language, while providing citations for checking. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | **Evidence 1:** BNM's Financial Capability and Inclusion Demand Side Survey 2024 found that financial knowledge rose from 60.2 in 2021 to 62.9 in 2024, while financial behaviour declined from 61.9 to 59.9. This indicates a continuing need for financial education.<br>**Source / citation:** Bank Negara Malaysia, *Annual Report 2024*, pp. 55–59. [Official report section](https://www.bnm.gov.my/documents/20124/17493532/ar2024_en_box4.pdf).<br><br>**Evidence 2:** The National Strategy for Financial Literacy 2026–2030 identifies the need to build students' everyday money management and longer-term planning skills before they enter employment.<br>**Source / citation:** Financial Education Network, *Malaysia National Strategy for Financial Literacy 2026–2030*, pp. 26–27. [Official strategy](https://www.fenetwork.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FEN_NS2_ENG_Interactive_FA_LowRes.pdf).<br><br>These findings support the educational need. They do not prove that a chatbot will change users' financial behaviour. Difficulty navigating PDFs is an initial project assumption that can be explored through user feedback. |
| **Initial Related Work (at least 3)** | **Study / solution 1: MoneyMama.** A Malaysian personal money assistant with conversational support and financial tools.<br>**Source / citation:** [MoneyMama official website](https://moneymama.co/).<br>**Key limitation:** The public website provides insufficient technical detail to assess retrieval and citation performance against this project's selected PDFs. This is a limitation of the available comparison evidence, not proof that the product lacks these capabilities.<br><br>**Study / solution 2: AKPK MyBijak Kewangan.** A portal offering financial health assessments, learning modules, financial articles and budget simulators.<br>**Source / citation:** [Government of Malaysia service description](https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/digital-services/mybijak-kewangan-financial-management).<br>**Key limitation:** The listed tools and resources serve separate learning and planning tasks; they do not by themselves offer the proposed question-and-answer experience across selected PDFs. No claim is made about unverified technical features.<br><br>**Study / solution 3: PIDM Emergency Savings Calculator.** A structured tool for estimating emergency savings needs and the time required to reach a target.<br>**Source / citation:** [PIDM official calculator](https://www.pidm.gov.my/finlit/pidm-emergency-savings-calculator).<br>**Key limitation:** It focuses on one type of calculation rather than answering open questions across financial education topics. |
| **Engineering Gap / Limitation** | **Gap / Limitation:** There is room for a small educational system whose document selection, retrieval process, citations and test results can be inspected as part of a student engineering project. FinAI Guide will bring approved Malaysian PDF content into one question-and-answer interface and test whether its answers are correct and supported by the cited passages. This is a practical engineering opportunity, not a claim that no similar system exists.<br><br>**Why use AI:** Students may ask the same concept in different ways. RAG can match a question to relevant passages and generate a connected explanation, including information from more than one passage when needed. A search tool can locate documents, while this project also aims to explain their content; a foundation model will be used without training a new language model from scratch.<br><br>**Initial boundary:** The system will provide general education only. It will not recommend financial products, give personalised financial or investment advice, analyse bank statements, process receipts, classify expenses or make decisions for users. Its knowledge will be limited to the approved documents, and it will acknowledge missing evidence. Implementation and evaluation are planned, not completed. |
| **Initial Project Objectives (2–3)** | **O1:** To design a RAG architecture and a PDF processing pipeline that preserves document titles, source links and page information.<br>**O2:** To develop a financial education chatbot that retrieves relevant passages, provides answers with citations and handles unsupported questions and personalised advice requests appropriately.<br>**O3:** To evaluate retrieval relevance, answer correctness, citation support, response time and safe handling using a defined set of test questions. |
| **Target Users** | Malaysian university students and young adults who want clear explanations of basic budgeting, saving, emergency funds and general money management. The initial version will focus on English questions and will not require a personal financial profile. |
| **Data Sources (at least 2)** | **Source 1:** MFPC, *My Money & Me Guide*, first edition, 2024.<br>**URL:** [Official PDF](https://www.mfpc.org.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Guide-Book-New.pdf).<br>**Planned use:** General financial education passages that fit the approved scope.<br><br>**Source 2:** FEN, *Financial Literacy Core Competencies for Malaysian Adults*.<br>**URL:** [Official PDF](https://www.fenetwork.my/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/FLCC-for-Malaysian-adults_compressed.pdf).<br>**Planned use:** Relevant budgeting, money management and saving content. As this is a competency framework, its usefulness for answering questions must be checked rather than assumed.<br><br>**Source 3:** EPF/KWSP, *Belanjawanku 2026*.<br>**URL:** [Publisher PDF link](https://www.kwsp.gov.my/documents/d/guest/booklet-belanjawanku-2026).<br>**Publication listing:** [Universiti Malaya SWRC](https://swrc.um.edu.my/related-news-amp-publications-belanjawanku).<br>**Planned use:** Explanations of expenditure categories and published estimates, keeping the year, city and household category attached to each figure.<br><br>**Validation status:** Sources 1 and 2 were accessible as PDFs during source checking. Source 3 is listed by SWRC, but its PDF download and extraction have not been confirmed. Local extraction quality, topic coverage and permitted use still need checking for all sources before indexing. Record source URLs and document versions; do not publish the original PDFs unless redistribution is allowed. |
| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Both systems will answer questions using a controlled collection of public documents. Both will retrieve relevant passages before generating an answer, provide citations and apply safeguards when the evidence is insufficient. Their evaluation will examine answer quality, supporting evidence, response time and safe handling. |
| **Different from EduAdvisor** | EduAdvisor focuses on university courses, prerequisites and graduation requirements. FinAI Guide focuses on everyday financial education for Malaysian young adults. It must distinguish educational explanations from personalised financial advice and preserve the context of any published financial figures. FinAI Guide is an independent AI Engineering semester project and is separate from my FinAI FYP expense monitoring system. |
| **Responsible AI Risks (at least 2)** | **Risk 1 — Hallucination and misleading citations:** The model may invent a financial fact or cite a page that does not support its answer. Students could misunderstand a concept and use the explanation when making money decisions. **Planned control:** Ground answers in retrieved passages, retain page metadata, check citation support during evaluation and acknowledge missing evidence.<br><br>**Risk 2 — Unsafe personalised advice:** A user may ask which investment to buy or request a financial plan based on their circumstances. An unsuitable answer could cause financial harm. **Planned control:** Keep responses educational, avoid product recommendations and direct users to qualified professionals when personal advice is needed.<br><br>**Risk 3 — Privacy:** Users may enter account numbers, identity numbers or other sensitive details. These could be exposed through stored conversations or logs. **Planned control:** Do not require these details or accept personal document uploads; minimise logging and check privacy handling with synthetic examples.<br><br>**Risk 4 — Outdated or unequal coverage:** Older documents or examples from selected household types and locations may not reflect every user's situation. **Planned control:** Identify document versions and the context of figures, explain coverage limits and test questions across the main approved topics. |
